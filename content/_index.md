Latin is learnt here in order to be read. The course takes a learner from the
alphabet and vowel length to unadapted classical prose and verse, by way of the
morphology and syntax that make an inflected sentence legible. It is written for
pupils working towards the *Latinum* and for adults who come to Latin for
history, law, medicine, theology or philology.
{{< /lead >}}
{{< /hero >}}

## What this course is

A **reading course**, and it says so plainly. Latin has no living speech
community: there is no one to interrupt, no service counter to negotiate, no
register to judge against native intuition. The competences a modern language
course spends most of its time on — spoken interaction, oral production,
sociolinguistic appropriateness — have no target here. Rather than pretend
otherwise, the course states which parts of the Common European Framework it
implements and which it declares out of scope, and gives its reasons.

What remains is not a remainder. It is the whole of **reception** (reading), the
whole of **linguistic competence** (morphology, syntax, vocabulary,
orthography), and the whole of **mediation** — translating and expounding a text
for a reader who cannot reach it unaided. That last is the characteristic Latin
skill, and the framework has proper descriptors for it.

## How it is organised

Progression is grammatical and lexical: declensions before tenses, tenses before
the subjunctive, the subjunctive before indirect statement, scansion last. Each
stage is crosswalked to a CEFR **reading** level rather than driven by one.

{{< card >}}
**[Ianua — the doorway](/ianua/)** · pre-A1r
The alphabet, vowel length and macrons, syllable division and stress, and an
honest side-by-side of restored classical and ecclesiastical pronunciation.
{{< /card >}}
{{< card >}}
**[Stage I — Tirones](/stage-i-tirones/)** · A1r
First and second declension, first and second conjugation, *esse*, and the
sentence patterns of adapted Latin narrative.
{{< /card >}}
{{< card >}}
**[Stage II — Discipuli](/stage-ii-discipuli/)** · A2r
The remaining declensions, adjectives and pronouns, and the full indicative
tense system in both voices.
{{< /card >}}
{{< card >}}
**[Stage III — Lectores](/stage-iii-lectores/)** · B1r
Participles, the ablative absolute, indirect statement and the first uses of the
subjunctive, on lightly adapted authors.
{{< /card >}}
{{< card >}}
**[Stage IV — Auctores](/stage-iv-auctores/)** · B2r
Full syntax, gerund and gerundive, conditional sentences and scansion, on
unadapted Caesar, Cicero, Ovid and Vergil.
{{< /card >}}
{{< card >}}
**[Appendices](/appendices/)**
Reference rather than instruction: orthography, pronunciation, morphology
tables, core vocabulary, syntax, scansion and the translation rubric.
{{< /card >}}

Every unit follows the same five moves — Activate, Input, Practise, Apply,
Reflect — and carries differentiated exercises with answer keys and teacher
notes. Slide decks and worksheets are generated from the shared LaTeX templates
and committed as editable source alongside the PDF; see
[Materials](/materials/).

## Texts and licences

Latin's corpus is entirely in the public domain. Where sister courses in this
family must commission or license their input, this one can quote Caesar,
Cicero, Vergil, Ovid, Catullus and the Vulgate freely, from a cited edition,
from the first page on which a learner is ready for them. Adapted passages are
marked as adaptations; quoted passages carry their source.

Course content is licensed **CC BY-SA 4.0**; the site code is **MIT**. See

declaration and the editorial conventions.
