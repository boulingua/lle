{{< lead >}}
*Lectores* are readers. This is the stage at which Latin stops being a sequence
of finite clauses and becomes what it actually is: a language that subordinates
by participle, by infinitive and by mood, and that expects the reader to hold a
sentence open until its verb arrives. It is the hardest stage, and the one after
which most learners can be left alone with a text.
{{< /lead >}}
{{< /hero >}}

## The grammatical spine

| Units | Morphology | Syntax |
|---|---|---|
| 21–22 | Present, perfect and future participles | Participial phrases; the participle as a subordinate clause |
| 23 | — | The ablative absolute, and how to translate it four ways |
| 24–25 | Infinitives, all tenses, active and passive | Indirect statement: accusative and infinitive |
| 26–27 | Present and imperfect subjunctive | Purpose clauses with *ut*/*nē*; indirect command |
| 28–29 | Perfect and pluperfect subjunctive | *Cum* clauses; result clauses; indirect question |
| 30–31 | Deponent and semi-deponent verbs | Impersonal constructions |
| 32 | Irregular verbs: *ferō*, *eō*, *volō*, *fīō* | Revision and consolidation on continuous text |

The ablative absolute and indirect statement are given their own units because
they are the two constructions on which unaided reading actually turns, and
because both require the learner to abandon the word-by-word habit that carried
them through Stages I and II.

## Reading and mediation

From this stage the passages are drawn from real authors and only lightly
adapted: Caesar's clearer narrative, Cicero's letters, Nepos, the *Vulgate* where
its Latin is genuinely simple. Every passage names its edition, and every
adaptation says what it changed.

Translation becomes an examined skill rather than a by-product. The stage
introduces the **translation rubric** — an appendix that states what is being
marked when a translation is marked: accuracy of morphology, recovery of
sentence structure, and the register of the English or German produced. It is the
core mediation competence of the course, and it is assessed against a published
scale rather than against a teacher's ear.

## Assessment

**Probatio III** closes the stage, with a mid-stage check after Unit 26. From
this stage the examinations include an unseen passage with a vocabulary list, on
the principle that a reading course must at some point test reading rather than
recall.
