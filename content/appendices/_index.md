The appendices are where the course keeps everything a learner needs to look up
rather than to work through. They are versioned, cited and linked from the units
that depend on them, and they are written to be read on their own.
{{< /lead >}}
{{< /hero >}}

## Available now

{{< card-grid >}}
{{< card >}}
**[Orthography and macrons](orthography-and-macrons/)**
The editorial policy of the course: when vowels are marked long and when they are
not, how *u/v* and consonantal *i* are printed, what happens to the *ae* and *oe*
digraphs, and how quoted inscriptions are treated.
{{< /card >}}
{{< card >}}
**[Pronunciation](pronunciation/)**
Restored classical and ecclesiastical pronunciation set out side by side, with
the evidence for the reconstruction, the syllable and stress rules, and a note on
why this course ships no synthetic audio.

## Planned

These are written alongside the stages that first need them, so that no appendix
is published before there is a unit that links to it.

- **Morphology reference** — every declension and conjugation, with principal
  parts, in one place. Begun with Stage I, completed with Stage III.
- **Core vocabulary** — the high-frequency list the course draws on, ordered by
  yield rather than alphabetically, with the stage at which each item is
  introduced.
- **Glossary** — every Latin word used in the course, with the unit of first
  appearance.
- **Syntax and constructions** — the reference counterpart to Stage III: ablative
  absolute, indirect statement, the subjunctive clauses, conditional sentences.
- **Scansion guide** — the dactylic hexameter and the elegiac couplet, written
  for Stage IV.
- **Civilisation notes** — the household, the city, the political year, and the
  book, as short essays keyed to the units that raise them.
- **Translation rubric** — the published scale against which mediation tasks are
  marked, from Stage III onward.
- **Teaching workflow** — how the materials are meant to be used in a
  double lesson, and what to cut when there is only one.
