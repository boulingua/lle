## The set texts

The whole corpus is in the public domain, and the course uses that freely.
Passages are quoted from a cited public-domain edition — the Perseus Digital
Library, the *Packard Humanities Institute* Latin texts, and the older Teubner and
Oxford texts whose copyright has lapsed — with the edition recorded on the page.

- **Caesar**, *Dē bellō Gallicō* I — the campaign narrative, for its
  unforgiving but regular period structure.
- **Cicero**, *In Catilīnam* I — the opening of the first speech, for
  rhetorical Latin at its most quotable and most syntactically dense.
- **Ovid**, *Metamorphōsēs* — a self-contained tale, for narrative hexameter.
- **Vergil**, *Aenēis* II or IV — an extended passage, for verse that must be
  scanned to be understood.

Where a passage is genuinely difficult, the difficulty is named. The course does
not simplify Vergil and call the result Vergil.

## Scansion

Metre is treated as a reading skill, not an ornament. A learner who cannot scan
cannot tell *ā* from *a* in a text printed without macrons, and cannot hear the
line. The scansion guide is written for this stage and is used from Unit 38
onwards.

## Assessment

**Probatio IV** closes the stage: an unseen prose passage with a vocabulary list,
a passage of set-text verse with scansion and comment, and a translation marked
against the published rubric. At this point the course's conformance declaration
extends to **B2 reception**.

{{< callout type="note" title="Beyond Stage IV" >}}
A fifth stage — *Periti*, at C1 reading — is declared as a stretch: literary and
stylistic analysis across a wider canon, including the supplementary
ecclesiastical and medieval reading track. It is honestly aspirational and is
not part of the committed plan; nothing in Stages 0–IV depends on it.
