{{< lead >}}
*Tirones* are recruits. This stage builds the smallest apparatus with which a
continuous Latin sentence can be read: two declensions, two conjugations, the
verb *esse*, and the habit of finding the verb before deciding what the
nominative is doing. The Latin is adapted; the syntax is real.
{{< /lead >}}
{{< /hero >}}

## The grammatical spine

| Units | Morphology | Syntax |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | First declension, singular and plural; *esse*, present indicative | Nominative and predicate nominative; negation with *nōn*; *et*, *sed*, *quoque* |
| 3–4 | Second declension, masculine and neuter; adjectives of the first and second declension | Attributive and predicative agreement; the genitive of possession |
| 5–6 | First and second conjugation, present indicative active | The accusative direct object; verb-final word order; *quis?* *quid?* |
| 7–8 | Imperfect indicative; prepositions | Place with *in* and the ablative, motion with *ad* and the accusative; the dative of the indirect object |
| 9–10 | Vocative and imperative; the present infinitive | Complementary infinitive; direct questions with *-ne*, *nōnne*, *num* |

The order is not the order of a grammar book. The accusative waits until Unit 5
because there is no transitive verb before then to govern it, and adjectives wait
until Unit 3 because a first-declension adjective beside a first-declension noun
teaches a learner nothing about agreement. Each form is introduced at the point
where the reading requires it.

Vocabulary is drawn from a published high-frequency core, so that a word learnt
in Stage I is a word that will keep paying: roughly two hundred and fifty items
across the stage, chosen for their yield in the authors read later rather than
for the convenience of the sentence in hand.

## The storyworld

Units follow one household — a *paterfamilias* and his *matrōna*, their children,
and a Greek *paedagōgus* who narrates and who is, throughout, less free and more
literate than the people he serves. The setting is the late Republic. Keeping one
cast across ten units means vocabulary recurs without contrivance, and the
civilisation thread of this stage — the Roman house and the shape of a Roman day
— has somewhere to live.

## Assessment

Each unit ends with a short check of its own, with an answer key and a marking
scale. The stage as a whole is closed by **Probatio I**, a written examination
attached to the capstone unit, testing reading comprehension, morphological
recognition and a translation into the metalanguage. The scaffold currently
carries the unit-level check for Unit 1; the stage Probatio is written once the
ten units are complete.

{{< callout type="note" title="What A1r does and does not mean" >}}
A1r is a **reading** level, crosswalked to the framework's reception scales. It
does not claim that a learner can hold a conversation, and no descriptor in this
stage asks them to. The scales this course implements, and the ones it declares
out of scope for a classical language, are listed in
[About this course](/about/).
