{{< lead >}}
*Discipuli* are pupils rather than recruits: past the drill, not yet at the
authors. This stage completes the noun — third, fourth and fifth declensions —
and completes the indicative, so that by its end every finite verb form a learner
meets in a narrative text can be named and parsed.
{{< /lead >}}
{{< /hero >}}

## The grammatical spine

| Units | Morphology | Syntax |
|---|---|---|
| 11–12 | Third declension, consonant and *i*-stems | Ablative of description; apposition |
| 13 | Fourth and fifth declensions | Expressions of time: *quō tempore*, *quam diū* |
| 14–15 | Third declension adjectives; comparison of adjectives and adverbs | Ablative of comparison; *quam* with the superlative |
| 16–17 | Personal, demonstrative and relative pronouns | Relative clauses; antecedent and agreement |
| 18–19 | Perfect, pluperfect and future perfect active | Sequence of past events in narrative |
| 20 | The passive voice, present and perfect systems | Ablative of the agent with *ā/ab* |

The third declension is the pivot of the whole stage, and it is taught the way it
actually behaves: from the genitive singular, because the nominative lies. Two
units are spent on it, and its gender rules are learnt as tendencies with named
exceptions rather than as a table to be trusted.

## The storyworld

The household leaves the house. This stage follows the *paterfamilias* into
public life — clientship and the morning *salūtātiō*, the courts, an election —
and the civilisation thread turns from domestic arrangement to the machinery of
the late Republic. The narrator remains the *paedagōgus*, whose view of Roman
politics is that of a man who cannot vote in it.

## Reading

Passages remain adapted, but the adaptation becomes lighter and begins to be
declared: from Unit 16 each reading passage names the ancient text it was made
from, and the closing units set a short unadapted sentence or two beside the
adapted paragraph so that the distance can be seen.

## Assessment

**Probatio II** closes the stage, attached to the capstone unit. From this stage
onward a **mid-stage check** falls after the fifth unit, testing only the
morphology introduced so far — a cheap, early signal that the third declension has
or has not landed.
